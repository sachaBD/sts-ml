Checkpoint anatomy • Act 2 selected incumbent

What does the overworld network actually see?

It can express card synergies—but only after compressing the deck. Each card is encoded independently, then all cards are summed and averaged. A nonlinear value head can learn that combinations matter. There are no card–card edges, no attention weights, and no combat rollout inside this network.

HP-bin and policy heads exist but are not trained by the Act 2 learner and are not used for these decisions.

The output is expected progress score, not a calibrated win probability. This checkpoint uses floors: 0.4 × floor/33 + 0.2 × Act 1 clear + 0.4 × Act 2 clear. Learning uses TD(λ=0.7). A sigmoid output of 0.60 is not “60% Act 2 clear”.

2. Does it respond to card combinations?
This heatmap measures non-additivity of the learned value logit when two unupgraded cards are added to the same recorded deck:
I(A,B) = L(deck+A+B) − L(deck+A) − L(deck+B) + L(deck)


Green = more favorable together than their separate additions suggest; red = less favorable together. Diagonal = adding two copies. Hover for mean ± 1 standard error across the sampled contexts.


These are model probes, not measured gameplay synergy. Deck-size/mean-pooling effects, generic curvature and route switching can all contribute. Logits avoid sigmoid-only curvature, but do not remove these other effects. Modified decks may be outside the training distribution. The sample is deterministic, non-random and from collection data—not held-out evaluation; SE only describes variability across these sampled contexts.

5. A structural blind spot: route order disappears
These routes have identical room counts and the same occupied floor positions, but the campfire and elite trade places. The network maps them to effectively identical vectors.
Σf [e(roomf) + e(f)] = Σf e(roomf) + Σf e(f)
The floor terms do not bind a room to its position. At a fixed remaining-floor mask, the encoder sees a bag of rooms. Thus “rest before elite” and “elite before rest” are indistinguishable to this path encoder. The worker still enumerates legal connected routes, and path after-states can differ in other features; this is not a claim that the entire route chooser is identical in every case.
First architecture change I would test: bind room to floor before pooling (e.g. a learned room×floor token table, or per-slot MLP over concatenated embeddings). Keep the selected checkpoint frozen; introduce a new architecture kind/spec, retrain, and compare gameplay on paired held-out seeds.


6. What is captured—and what is missing?
SignalCurrent selected topology
Card identities, upgrades, duplicatesYes: context-conditioned card embeddings; sum and mean pools
Card × card / card × relicPossible implicitly in the pooled nonlinear head; no explicit pair encoder
Current boss / HP / progressionYes: boss embedding + scalars; boss/HP/floor also condition cards
Mechanics: damage, block, energy, exhaust tagsNo explicit mechanic features; must infer their relevance from card IDs and training returns
Hand, draw order, intents, playable sequencesNot inputs to this overworld net; the separate combat agent handles combat
Route compositionYes: room-count summary per legally enumerated route
Route order / room at specific floorNo: additive room + floor pooling destroys the association
Coordinates / burning elite / explicit act numberNot encoded here; boss identity and absolute floor provide indirect act information


Recommendation: fix the map information loss and improve targets before assuming a larger net or attention will win. A small deck-attention experiment is still reasonable for synergy, but judge it on equal-cost gameplay—not just loss or appealing heatmaps.
