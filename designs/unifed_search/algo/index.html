Part 0 · Vocabulary

Plain-English Glossary

Term Meaning
Eval One neural-network forward pass on one state (returns value, logits, and thinking). A batch of 32 states counts as 32 evals.
Particle One concrete sample of hidden state (shuffled draw-pile order) and future RNG stream consistent with what the player can currently see (combat::particles(...)).
Shared / Paired Particles Testing competing moves on the exact same particle indices (particles[0], particles[1], …). Lucky/unlucky draws hit both moves together and cancel out in Q_p(B) - Q_p(A).
Public DAG (Directed Acyclic Graph) Search graph keyed by order-invariant public observation (observation_key), so different card sequences reaching the same visible state merge into a single shared node.
Transposition Two move orders (e.g. Strike → Defend vs. Defend → Strike) arriving at the exact same public node.
Plunge Fast-forwarding mid-turn choices using the network's greedy argmax prior (logits) until reaching the next turn start (Turn+1) or fight end.
VOI (Value of Information) Expected gain in fight win probability of our committed root move from buying a specific block of extra evals (computed via Laplace/Exponential Expected Improvement, EI).
Aleatoric vs. Epistemic Aleatoric = draw/roll luck across particles (reduced by adding shared particles). Epistemic = blind spots from following a greedy plunge without branching mid-turn choices (reduced by branching nodes with high thinking or prior uncertainty).
Part 1 · Raw Ingredients

What 1 NN Evaluation Gives Us

Every time the GPU evaluates a combat state s with K deduplicated legal actions, a single trunk pass emits three heads at once. No extra forward pass is ever needed to gate or score a node.

Part 2 · The Core Insight

Two Reasons a Move Can Beat the Current Leader

~96% of Slay the Spire decisions don't change under deep search. In the ~4% where the prior move A is wrong, our estimate of challenger e vs. leader â is uncertain for two orthogonal reasons:

1. Aleatoric Uncertainty (Particle / Draw Noise)

Playing Pommel Strike draws different hidden cards in Particle 0 vs. Particle 1.

  • Fix: evaluate contenders on shared particles (PARTICLES block) + subtract particle difficulty β_p.
  • Limit: sampling 1,000 particles never fixes a systematically bad mid-turn continuation!

2. Epistemic Uncertainty (Greedy Plunge Error)

Challenger e (Offering) draws 5 cards into mid-turn state u, where the greedy prior blunders.

  • Signal: τ(u) predicts how much V(u) rises when u branches off its greedy prior.
  • Fix: buy BRANCH(e, u)—spending interior evals only where branching can lift e past â.

Part 4 · Inside the Public DAG

Why Shared Transpositions Cost Zero Extra Thinking

In Slay the Spire, card-order permutations (Strike → Defend vs. Defend → Strike) merge into the exact same public observation_key. Differential reachability across particles δ⁺_u(e) = max(0, ω_e(u) - ω_â(u)) prices this automatically:

In Case A, improving u_shared lifts Q(â) and Q(e) by the exact same amount—it cannot flip the root move, so VOI is 0 (if the fight reaches u_shared, we get to think there on the next decision). In Case B, only challenger e visits u_hot, so upward revision τ(u_hot) directly narrows the 0.04 root gap.

Part 6 · Training & Exploration

How One Algorithm Plays, Explores, and Trains Itself

An autonomous RL agent has to solve three jobs with one search engine: play strongly in evaluation, generate diverse fights in self-play, and measure what deeper search gains to train ThinkingHead. Two simple dials—compute price λ and root sampling noise gumbel_scale—configure all three:

Because block selection (argmax VOI / Cost) does not depend on λ, Evaluation at price λ stops on an exact prefix of the trajectory that the λ = 0 Probe runs and grades—so ThinkingHead always predicts the true payoff of our actual play search.

Two Distinct Exploration Signals (and Why We Need Both)

Self-training requires exploring in two different places: inside a search tree (so probes find combos the head hasn't learned yet) and across self-play fights (so fights visit diverse states). One is deterministic; the other is random:

A. Inside a Search: Deterministic Floor τ_floor(u)

What if ThinkingHead is freshly initialized (0 everywhere) or misses a novel mid-turn combo and predicts thinking(u) ≤ 0?

τ₀(u) = max( max_k g_u[k], ½·s₀·√(v(1-v))·(1 - π[greedy]) )

  • Deterministic (same state → same probe tree).
  • Only on interior nodes: root OPEN uses pure g_root[k] when λ > 0, so 50/50 card-order ties still exit at 1 eval in play, while shared transpositions inside the DAG cancel via δ⁺_u = 0.

B. Across Fights: Random Noise gumbel_scale · G_e

On the 15/16 non-probe moves in self-play, playing deterministic argmax would repeat identical games from the same start.

z_e = ℓ_max + k₀(S(e) - V₀);   play argmax(z_e + scale·G_e)

  • Random across fights (G_e ~ Gumbel(0, 1)).
  • Exact at both extremes: when N_e = 0 (1-eval skip), z_e ≡ logits[e] (samples from raw prior); when searched, samples from improved softmax(z) while keeping gumbel_scale = 0 in Evaluation & Probes.

Complete Parameter Table

Category Parameter Default / Setting Purpose

gumbel_scale 1.0 Self-Play · 0.0 Eval & Probe Gumbel noise scale on root improved logits z_e for cross-fight trajectory exploration.

3. Wave Shapes (2) wave_particles 2–4 Shared particles batched together per GPU wave.
